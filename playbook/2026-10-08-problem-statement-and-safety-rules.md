# Problem Statement and Safety Rules

*Phase 1 of the Move Smart playbook. Draft for review.*

## The problem

Choosing a new city and planning a move means juggling a lot at once: comparing places, building a budget, and sorting out logistics. This playbook is a guide for anyone considering a relocation. AI can do the heavy lifting, while you stay in charge of the decisions.

## What AI is used for

| Area | How AI can help | Who makes the final call |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **City comparison** | Suggest criteria, summarize public data, organize pros and cons | Me, after checking the sources |
| **Budget** | Build a template, list costs people often forget, explain how estimates work | Me, using current prices from real listings and quotes |
| **Logistics** | Draft timelines and checklists, compare moving options at a high level | Me, after confirming details with the companies involved |

## Safety rules

### Never put into an AI tool

- **Personal finances:** income, savings, debts, account details, credit information. Use ranges or example numbers instead (for instance, "a $2,500 rent budget" as a placeholder, not my real figures).
- **Personal information:** full name with address, government ID numbers, phone numbers, email addresses, employer details, or anything that points to my exact home or workplace.
- **Other people's private information:** friends, family, neighbors, or anyone else who hasn't agreed to be part of this.
- **Anything I wouldn't be comfortable seeing on a public page.**

### Always double-check

| What | Why it needs a second look | How to check it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Neighborhood and safety claims** | AI can repeat outdated information or stereotypes about an area, and safety is too important to take on faith | Official local crime data, city and police department resources, recent local news, and visiting in person |
| **Rent and cost figures** | Prices change quickly, and AI may be working from old data | Current listings, quotes, and official sources, with the date noted |
| **Numbers, dates, and rules** | AI can state things confidently that turn out to be wrong | The original source, such as a government site or the company itself |
| **Anything that sounds too neat** | A clean, confident answer can hide missing details | Ask for sources, then read them |

### A note on neighborhood safety

Safety is the highest-stakes area in this project, so it gets the strictest rule: **AI can help me ask good questions about a neighborhood, but it never gets the final word on whether somewhere is safe.** I use it to decide what to look into, then confirm with official data and my own visit.

## How I'll know the rules are working

- The test log records every time AI got something wrong, and whether I caught it.
- No private details appear anywhere in this repo.
- Every important number in the dashboard has a source and a date.
